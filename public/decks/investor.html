Investor brief · the business case

Faster car-loan decisions, at a cost per file a lender can plan around

cercit drafts the credit appraisal for new-car loans; the lender’s own policy decides. This brief covers who pays, how much, and when it turns a profit.

November 2026 · Early stage · Product built and tested on synthetic data · No lender revenue yet

The cost today

Every car-loan file is built by hand, and lenders pay for it in people and lost customers

25–40

credit memos an officer builds by hand each working day, re-typing bureau, bank and salary data

₹95

officer cost per file, at ₹7 lakh a year and about 7,500 files a year (our assumption)

Days

of waiting while documents go back and forth; some buyers go to another lender

The salary is the smaller cost. The bigger ones are slow decisions, two officers reading one file two ways, and reasons pieced together for the auditor later.

Investor brief · 02 · Workload from the founder’s years in vehicle finance; officer cost is an assumption

What a lender gets

Four things a credit head can take to the board

Decisions in minutes, not days

Documents are read and cross-checked as they arrive. Clean files go straight to approval.

One policy, applied the same way

The lender’s own rules run on every file. The credit head proposes changes; an admin approves them.

An audit trail that can’t be edited

Each decision records its policy version and reasons. The log is hash-chained and copied to locked storage nightly.

Officers on exceptions only

The same team handles more files and spends its time on the cases that need judgement.

Who buys

Mid-size lenders that finance cars but don’t build their own systems

Vehicle NBFCs

Car loans are core business, decisions by branch officers, and pressure to decide faster than banks.

Small finance banks

Growing retail books, small technology teams, strict audit and RBI reporting needs.

Co-operative banks

Lending to salaried members, mostly on manual files, with no budget for a custom build.

Large private and public banks build in-house, so they are not our first buyers. That is why the 3-year target is four lenders, not a share of the whole market.
